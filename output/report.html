PACE/CHECK01 · Executive summary

The case in one page

Training logs are plentiful.
A useful picture is not.

A first HYROX race can leave an athlete with one confusing question: “I trained hard—why didn’t race day feel like training?” PACE/CHECK is built to make preparation easier to review, without pretending a score can predict the finish.

What is live today

  • Workout-first station picker with single or multi-station focus.
  • Local workout log, optional calorie entry, recovery check-in, and editable race setup.
  • Transparent readiness heuristic and a read-only weekly AI coach with two summary tools.
  • Upcoming race snapshot with official source links; HYROX Miami Beach is set for March 26–28, 2027.
  • HYROX Pulse: public Reddit search feed plus source-linked searches for X, Instagram, YouTube, and HYROX official.

PACE/CHECK02 · Origin & problem

The athlete’s starting point

“I felt prepared.
My body said otherwise.”

After training for a solo HYROX, the athlete crossed the line in 1:52. The target was 1:45 or faster—a seven-minute gap. Training, meals, and effort were present; a clear way to connect those inputs to race-specific preparation was not.

The friction

Workout details sit across memory, notes, wearables, and training apps. Station coverage and run-plus-station practice are hard to see in one glance.

The product question

Can we help an athlete notice training patterns and gaps early enough to ask better questions before race day?

The job to be done

“When I’m preparing for HYROX, help me keep a simple record of the work I’m doing, see which parts of the race I’ve practiced, and reflect on the trend without judging a missed day.”

This is a product hypothesis, not proof that the app would have changed the original result. A result depends on many factors—pacing, transitions, conditions, health, sleep, and the day itself—that this prototype cannot observe.

PACE/CHECK03 · Who it serves

One product, two entry points

Be welcoming to first-timers.
Useful to returning racers.

NEW TO HYROX

Start with the station picker

Choose one or several of the eight stations. The short guide explains what each involves, and plain-language effort choices avoid requiring training jargon.

Success looks like: getting started, learning the race format, and recording a repeatable week.

EXPERIENCED ATHLETE

Use the trend and detail

Review sessions, station coverage, recovery check-ins, race setup, and an optional AI-generated weekly reflection.

Success looks like: spotting a pattern worth discussing with a coach or changing in the plan.

Design choices that lower the barrier

  • Workout selection is the first screen; it does not require completing a long setup flow first.
  • Multiple station selections are stored as one session with explicit focus areas.
  • The score is withheld until three recent sessions exist; empty data is not displayed as a poor score.
  • Calories are optional and shown only when entered. The app does not invent an estimate.
  • Accountability has flexible choices: done, move, rest, or skip—no streak penalty.
The interface favors clear actions over a wall of analytics. Deeper method detail remains available on the Readiness tab.

PACE/CHECK06 · Readiness index

A transparent coaching signal

Useful only when the inputs are visible.

The index is deterministic frontend code. The LLM does not choose, calculate, or modify the score. It summarizes records separately when the athlete asks for a weekly review.

FactorWeightCurrent ruleWhat it cannot say
Run volume25%Run and simulation minutes; cappedRace pace capacity or physiological readiness
Station coverage25%Distinct station types across recent sessionsLoad quality, form, or station skill
Race-specific work20%Simulation entries, cappedHow representative a simulation was
Consistency15%Training days in the recent windowWhether a rest day was appropriate
Recovery check-ins15%Self-reported sleep, energy, sorenessMedical readiness or injury risk
Index = Σ (factor score × factor weight)Values are capped, displayed from 0–100, and hidden until at least 3 sessions are logged in the last 28 days.

The threshold and factor scales are prototype heuristics chosen for transparency and implementation simplicity, not clinical or sports-science standards.

PACE/CHECK07 · Agent & tool design

Where language models help—and where they do not

The coach reflects. The code calculates.

ATHLETEQuestion + recent log
→
PYDANTICAIBounded, read-only review
→
TOOLSTraining + recovery summaries
Deterministic path: workout records → local score calculation → Overview/Readiness UI. This path works without a model key.

Agent tools

  • summarize_training groups sessions by week, totals minutes and effort, and reports station coverage.
  • summarize_recovery summarizes recent self-reported sleep, energy, and soreness. It does not diagnose.

Bounds in code

4model requests per run
2tool calls per run
450output-token limit

Agent retries are set to zero. The frontend sends at most 40 workouts and 14 recovery check-ins. Request text is capped at 500 characters; Pydantic validates the payload.

The agent may still need multiple model requests to call tools and respond. These limits constrain one run; they do not replace provider-level budget alerts or a production rate limiter.

Trust boundaries

Support behavior without overstating ability.

RiskCurrent controlRemaining limitation
Prompt injection in user question/log dataSystem prompt treats user inputs as untrusted; tools only summarizePrompting is not a complete security boundary; no red-team corpus yet
Medical overclaimCoach prompt prohibits diagnosis, medication advice, and “train through pain” recommendationsNo clinical validation or specialized safety classifier
Unbounded model use4 requests, 2 tool calls, 450 output tokens, zero retriesNo per-user rate limit or provider budget alarm in prototype
Secret exposureKey is server-side in ignored `.env`; `.env.example` contains placeholders onlyNo deployment secret manager configured
Sensitive training dataLocal browser storage; coach sends a bounded subset only when askedLocal storage is not encrypted, synced, or account-isolated
External social contentReddit parser strips markup and accepts Reddit-hosted URLs; other platforms open source pagesUpstream RSS can fail/rate-limit; no moderation guarantee
Safety copy: the index is a training reflection aid, not actual race readiness, injury risk, diagnosis, or expected finish time. Concerning symptoms warrant qualified care.

CORS allows local development origins. The prototype has no authentication, database, production webhook, wearable sync, or push reminder service. These are deployment prerequisites, not hidden capabilities.

PACE/CHECK11 · Impact & next steps

What success should mean

Measure better preparation behavior—not just engagement.

There is no evidence yet that the app improves finish time. A responsible pilot should first determine whether athletes log consistently, understand the indicator, and find the station coverage view useful.

Near-term measures

  • Weekly active athletes who log ≥1 session
  • Workout logging completion and time-to-log
  • Coverage of distinct station types
  • Calories with recorded source (wearable/manual)
  • Self-reported usefulness and clarity

Do not optimize alone

  • Daily streaks that pressure unsafe training
  • Readiness score movement without better data
  • Race-time prediction from sparse training logs
  • Social-feed engagement without content quality
