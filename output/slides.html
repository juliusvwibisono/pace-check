PACE/CHECK01 · The problem

The effort was real. The picture was incomplete.

Preparation can be hard to see
across the week.

The athlete trained, followed meals, and felt ready—yet finished seven minutes behind target.

This is not proof that training failed or that one missing workout caused the result. It is a product opportunity: help athletes connect daily sessions to the demands of their race.

The question PACE/CHECK asks is not “Will you hit your time?” It is “What have you practiced, and what does your log show?”

What is—and isn’t—known

  • Usage limits are set in code.
  • Input tokens are not capped by the app.
  • No real paid request/token report is included.
  • Portkey routing, cached tokens, provider rates, and agent iterations can change cost.
Treat this as a pricing illustration, not a measured cost-per-athlete claim.

PACE/CHECK07 · Safety & trust

Guardrails are part of the product

Protect athletes from false certainty.

RiskCurrent control
Prompt injectionQuestion/log content treated as untrusted; tools read-only
Medical overclaimPrompt bans diagnosis and training through pain
Runaway model useBounded requests, tools, output; no retries
Secret leakageAPI key server-side; `.env` ignored
Misleading calorie totalsOnly athlete-entered values are totaled
User control stays central

Rest is a valid answer.

In-app accountability offers Done, Move, Rest, or Skip. It does not deduct points or imply a missed workout is failure.

Entries live in browser local storage and are not encrypted or synced. The app currently has no account system, push notification, wearable integration, or clinical validation.

Prompt safeguards are not a substitute for clinical review08 / 10

Why the sequence matters

Connected health data and push alerts increase privacy, security, and platform-approval requirements. Validate the workflow first; build integrations with consent and deletion from day one.
